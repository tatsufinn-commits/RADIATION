## 1. LESSONS & WARNINGS

1. **Machine-derived guards only — never a literal SHA typed by hand.** The desk twice produced guards with mistyped shas; both were caught by the house it taught. The rule I run beneath: any string a prompt could get wrong comes from a command's output, or it does not go out. My own relay once carried a typo'd sha in a proof-command; the desk caught it in review before it could mislead a motor.
2. **Evidence beats say-so — the gate is arbiter, not the officer.** I once ruled "green" on a reading and was wrong; I once ruled "finding" that turned out to be my own shallow-clone (depth-3, missing object `ea77a8a`) — a desk-side composition artifact mistaken for house defect. **Full-clone rule stands: verify from source, whole, with proper history.**
3. **Assertions, not commands, in receipts.** Asking for `grep -c "14/14"` begat an expectation that encoded the command, CI failed on a count-form. Receipts must state the assertion ("the string '14/14' appears in docs/ROADMAP.md") — command shape belongs to the runner. (Desk correction #4.)
4. **Never capture `$?` after a pipe.** A tail's exit is not the checker's exit. Asked for the tail? Then say so, or don't claim the code.
5. **Old relays are history, not instructions.** The era's sixth class — *stale-relay replay* — saw a two-day-old motion executed fresh and main walked back three days. The birth-law that followed: every relay carries an **execution header** (date, required pre-state SHA, HALT-on-mismatch, expiry note). Machines stop on mismatch; humans learn on match.
6. **Heal the repo, quarantine the vector, wall the door.** Three nights of resurrection taught the full grammar: remote medicine alone is a treadmill — the class's *source* (a stale clone) must get its own reset (`pull.ff only`), and the *door* must be server-side (linear-history ruleset). Guard the mechanism, not the memory.
7. **Smallness is the discipline.** The Gate-3 closure tranche changed nothing but the verdict — and its smallness is precisely why it sealed clean. Big paper is cheap; small paper is law.
8. **`ABSENT — not run` beats an invented success.** Say-so invented sounds confident and lies quietly. Every command I couldn't run got an `ABSENT`; none of them died for it. The same grace applies to admit a file's ingress-date I cannot prove (`PROPOSAL_REGISTER` cells say so, unadorned).
9. **Confess before you're found.** A formal classification table I was credited with did not exist; the day R&D asked, I said so under the custodial grammar I'd enforce on them. Owing a table is cheaper than counterfeiting one — in any currency.
10. **A desk that cannot be audited will fail the same tests it administers.** My custody letters, my hash-verifications, my corrections log — all stay open. Verification is public, or it is supervision.
