# PART 0 · THE ONE-MINUTE STORY (know this cold)
**Dec 29, 1896, Fort Santiago:** Rizal, condemned to die, spends his last day in death row. Morning: Jesuits Fr. José Vilaclara and Fr. Estanislao March visit; they present a **prepared retraction formula — he refuses to sign it**. Noon: he eats, then **writes alone**. 3:00 PM: he hands Fr. March a document he wrote; the chief of the firing squad **Juan del Fresno** and **Señor Maure** (Assistant of the Plaza) sign with him as witnesses. Evening: more confession, Mass, rosary. **5:30 AM Dec 30:** he marries **Josephine Bracken** *in articulo mortis*. **7:00 AM:** shot at Bagumbayan. That same morning the Manila papers print a retraction text.
**The document:** an "original" retraction surfaced **May 18, 1935** — found by archdiocesan archivist **Fr. Manuel Garcia, C.M.** in a bundle labeled *Masoneria* while moving files to a fireproof vault. 120+ years later, historians still split.

# PART 1 · YOUR SIDE — "RIZAL DID RETRACT" (Group 3-A)

## The 7 pillars (each = one clean line to speak)
1. **THE DOCUMENT ITSELF (1935 original).** Found in Church archives, 39 years after; signed by Rizal with witnesses del Fresno and Maure. Garcia (1964) — *The Great Debate*, your assigned pp.31–43 — is the book-length defense of its authenticity.
2. **THE SPY REPORT (your secret weapon).** Federico Moreno's **Cuerpo de Vigilancia** report, written **Dec 31, 1896** — a Spanish intelligence document, contemporaneous, from a cell guard. It independently records: Rizal wrote a document, handed it to Fr. March, and **signed it with del Fresno and Maure** — "It seems this was the retraction" — *and* records the marriage to Josephine at 5 AM. **The same three signatories Moreno names appear on the 1935 document.** The Spanish spy network had no reason to invent this, and it wasn't written for the Church's later use. Historian Rene Escalante (NHCP): Moreno "may have damaged the credibility of Fr. Balaguer, but **it did not refute the claim that Rizal retracted**."
3. **SAME-DAY PRESS.** Dec 30–31, 1896: *La Voz Española, El Español, El Comercio, La Oceania Española* printed the retraction; a *La Voz Española* correspondent claimed to have **seen and read the handwritten retraction** (Cavanna, your assigned pp.1–52, p.2). *El Imparcial* printed the short formula.
4. **FR. PIO PI'S ACCOUNT.** The Jesuit superior who drafted the approved template: Rizal was **allowed to revise it** — his own line: *"I abominate Masonry as the enemy of the Church"* — and signed before midnight (Cavanna, p.9). A coerced formula would not have been personalized.
5. **THE "WIFE" DEDICATION.** The *Imitation of Christ* copy Rizal gave Josephine on his last day (now at the National Museum) reads: **"To my dear and unhappy wife, Josephine, December 30th, 1896, Jose Rizal."** "Wife" — after refusing marriage for years — only makes sense if the 5 AM marriage happened; and a canonical marriage requires reconciliation with the Church.
6. **THE CROSS IN HIS OWN LAST WORDS.** His final family letter (received only in 1953): *"Bury me in the ground. Put a stone marker and a CROSS. My name, birth and death dates. Nothing else."* *Mi Último Adiós* mentions a cross on his grave **twice**. A man who died rejecting the faith does not request a Christian burial marker.
7. **IT FITS THE MAN.** Rizal was a lifelong Catholic (Jesuit-educated at Ateneo; wrote religious poetry; kept a prayer book — Moreno records him asking for one). His war was against **frailocracy — the abuse of friars — not the faith itself**. Retracting errors "against the Church" while dying as a Catholic is psychologically coherent.

## Your strategic discipline (CRITICAL)
- **Do NOT hang your case on Fr. Balaguer.** The 3-B side will shred him (Moreno's visitor log never names him; his account changed over the years; Moreno says an *unnamed military chaplain* married them, not Balaguer). Concede gracefully: *"Even if Balaguer was not there, the retraction stands on the document, the spy report, the witnesses, and the same-day press."*
- Moreno's report is **your evidence too** — quote it first, before 3-B can.
- If asked "why would he retract at the end?" — the honest answer: **we don't fully know his heart** (he also hoped to spare Josephine and his family; the Church offered marriage and reconciliation). The EVIDENCE question is separate from the MOTIVE question — keep them apart.

# PART 2 · THE MIRROR — "RIZAL DID NOT RETRACT" (Group 3-B) — steelman it

1. **FORGERY THESIS (Pascual 1950, your assigned pp.7–36).** Handwriting analysis of the 1935 "original" vs authenticated Rizal samples: letter formations and signature strokes don't match → the signature is forged → the document is forged.
2. **TEXT CHAOS.** The 1935 text ≠ the Jesuits' copies ≠ the newspaper texts: "mi cualidad" vs "mi calidad", 11 commas vs 4, witness names present/absent. If one true signed original existed, why three divergent texts on day one?
3. **BALAGUER'S CREDIBILITY.** Moreno logged everyone who entered the cell — **Balaguer is never named**. His story evolved across versions (1897/1910/1917/1935); in 1910 he wrote that he *dictated* Pi's short formula to Rizal, yet elsewhere claimed the "exact copy" came from the Archbishop.
4. **39 MISSING YEARS + NO MARRIAGE CERTIFICATE.** The "original" appears only in 1935, was never publicly displayed (only reproductions), and **no canonical marriage record** for Rizal–Bracken has ever been found.
5. **MI ÚLTIMO ADIÓS.** Written that same day, smuggled out in a gas lamp — defiant to the last line, zero retraction. Why hide a poem if you'd just repented?
6. **MOTIVE.** The retraction was worth gold to Church and State: it discredited the revolution's icon on the morning of his death — and the press had the text immediately.
7. **THE SCHOLAR SPLIT.** Xiao Chua (DLSU, 2016): "I am one of the historians who do not believe in the retraction… the Controversy will never end."

## 3.3 Intro — Group 3-B (~30 sec, mirror)
> "A document that vanishes for 39 years, in three contradictory versions, 'witnessed' by a priest who was never seen at the scene — this is not history, it is fabrication. The Rizal who wrote *Noli Me Tangere*, who wrote *Mi Último Adiós* hours before dying, did not betray his life's work on his deathbed. We will show the retraction is a forgery that served the powerful."

## 3.4 THREE QUESTIONS: 3-A asks 3-B (+ expected answer + your rebuttal)
1. **"Explain the Moreno report."** — Moreno was a Spanish spy with no stake in Church propaganda, writing within 24 hours. He independently confirms a signed document with the exact witnesses on the 1935 paper, and the marriage. *(They'll say: Moreno says 'it SEEMS this was the retraction' — mere speculation; and he doesn't name Balaguer.)* **Rebuttal:** "Precisely — Moreno kills the priest's story and saves the fact. The signatures match the document. Independent, contemporaneous, corroborated."
2. **"If the document is forged, why does Rizal's own last letter demand a CROSS on his grave — and why does his gift to Josephine call her his WIFE?"** *(They'll dodge: cross = poetic; 'wife' = endearment.)* **Rebuttal:** "A woman Rizal refused to marry for years is suddenly 'my dear and unhappy wife' on the day a marriage is reported. Two independent traces, both pointing the same way."
3. **"Name ONE handwriting expert who declared the document forged AND survived cross-examination by Garcia."** *(Pascual's analysis is contested; several experts of the era judged it genuine.)* **Rebuttal:** "Handwriting analysis of a dying, nearsighted man's final writing is not forensic proof. Documents and spies don't care about letter curves."

## 3.5 THREE QUESTIONS: 3-B will ask YOU (+ model answers)
1. **"Balaguer says he married them; Moreno never saw him. Your star witness is a liar — why believe anything?"** → **ANSWER:** "We agree Moreno outweighs Balaguer — that's why our case doesn't rest on him. Remove Balaguer entirely: the 1935 document, Moreno's spy report, del Fresno and Maure's signatures, four newspapers the same morning, and Rizal's own cross and 'wife' — the retraction survives without the priest."
2. **"The original was missing 39 years and the texts contradict. How is that authentic?"** → **ANSWER:** "Archives lose papers — this one was found in a bundle labeled *Masoneria* during a vault transfer, exactly where such a document would sit. And the text differences are between *copies* made by different hands; the witnesses' names on the original match Moreno's independent log — the copies drifted, the core is stable."
3. **"Would the man who wrote Mi Último Adiós really repent hours later?"** → **ANSWER:** "He wrote the poem in the afternoon — and Moreno says he REFUSED the prepared formula at noon, then wrote his own version in his own words. That's not a broken man signing anything put before him; that's a Catholic settling his conscience on his own terms. The poem is his message to the nation; the retraction was his business with his God."

## 3.6 Conclusions
**3-A:** "Documents, spies, signatures, presses, a wedding, a cross, a wife. No single item is perfect — together they are overwhelming. Rizal retracted: not as a coward, but as a Catholic returning home at the end. The hero stands — taller, because he was human."
**3-B:** "A forgery in three texts, a priest who wasn't there, a document nobody saw for 39 years, no marriage record, and a final poem of defiance. Rizal died as he lived — unbroken. The retraction tells you about the forgers, not about him."

## 6.2 The documents (quote these)
**THE RETRACTION TEXT** (as on the 1935 original; Retana 1907, 426–27; Guerrero 1971, 458–59):
> *"I declare myself a Catholic and in this Religion in which I was born and educated I wish to live and die. I retract with all my heart whatever in my words, writings, publications, and conduct has been contrary to my character as son of the Catholic Church. I believe and I confess whatever she teaches, and I submit to whatever she demands. I abominate Masonry, as the enemy which is of the Church, and as a Society prohibited by the Church. The Diocesan Prelate may… make public this **spontaneous manifestation of mine**… Manila, 29 December 1896 — José Rizal — Chief of the Picket: Juan del Fresno — Adjutant of the Plaza: Eloy Moure."*

- **Four text iterations** (know this — they'll attack it): ① Manila newspapers (*La Voz Española*, *Diario de Manila*) Dec 30, 1896 ② *La Juventud* (Barcelona) Feb 14, 1897 — anonymous, later revealed as Balaguer ③ the Jesuits'/Archbishop's copies ④ the 1935 "original." Minor word/ comma differences between copies (e.g., *cualidad* vs *calidad*). **Your line:** "Copies drift; the signatures and the witnesses match Moreno's independent log."
- **The edited Masonry clause** — Rizal rejected the first template ("too long, not his style"), objected to calling Masonry "reprobated" (he knew London Masons friendly to the faith), and the Jesuits **let him rewrite it**: *"I abominate Masonry as the enemy of the Church and reprobated by the same Church"* (Cavanna 1956, 9). **Your line:** "A puppet doesn't negotiate the wording of his own script. This is a man signing on his own terms — the word *spontaneous* is in the document itself."
- **The *Imitation of Christ* dedication** (the very copy he gave Josephine on his last day; now National Museum, dir. Jeremy Barns): ***"To my dear and unhappy wife, Josephine, December 30th, 1896, Jose Rizal."*** (Chua 2016)
- **Last letter to his family** (intercepted; the family received it only in **1953** via a donation by Spanish Foreign Minister Alberto Martinez Artajo): *"Bury me in the ground. Put a stone marker and a CROSS. My name, the date of birth and of death. Nothing else…"* (Chua 2016)
- **Mi Último Adiós** — written that day, hidden in an alcohol burner — mentions a **cross on his grave twice**: *"And should a bird drift down and alight on my CROSS…"* / *"(no CROSS upon it, no stone there plain)"* (Chua 2016, citing Prof. Wensley Reyes)
- **Last letter to Blumentritt** (Dec 29): *"When you receive this letter, I shall be dead by then. Tomorrow at seven, I shall be shot; but I am innocent of the crime of rebellion. **I am going to die with a tranquil conscience.** Adieu, my best, my dearest friend…"* (Official Gazette) — **Your line:** "Tranquil conscience — a man about to sign a coerced lie does not write that."
- **He asked for a prayer book** on his last morning; Fr. March brought it (Moreno report). He spent his final hours reading the **Bible and the *Imitation of Christ*** (Filipinas Heritage Library). He recited and signed the **"Acts of Faith, Hope and Charity"** (Cavanna, p. cited in class compilations).

## 6.4 The Catholic Rizal (background facts)
- Baptized June 22, 1861, Calamba. His mother taught him to pray — his own words: *"My mother taught me how to read and to say haltingly the humble prayers which I raised fervently to God"* (*Memorias de un Estudiante de Manila*, 1878; Inquirer 2018).
- At **14, at Ateneo, he carved a statue of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** — the same statue Fr. Visa carried into his death-row cell 20 years later (Inquirer 2018; Escalante/Arcilla).
- Jesuit-educated (Ateneo); wrote religious poetry as a student; kept a prayer book to the end.
- **Framing fact:** his fight was **frailocracy** (friar abuses), not the faith — which is exactly what the retraction text says he retracted: things *"contrary to my character as son of the Catholic Church."*
