## Claims
1. **Drivers/growth of Big Data** — 1990s terabytes → 2000s petabytes → 2010s exabytes; sources: genomic sequencing & diagnostic imaging, photos/video on WWW, video surveillance, mobile geospatial + metadata, smart devices/sensors (smart grid/buildings), nontraditional IT (RFID, GPS, seismic); new data economy (data vendors, data cleaners, crowdsourcing). [D] (EMC)
2. **FOUR PLAYER GROUPS in the ecosystem** — ① Data DEVICES (gather/generate data; per 1 GB new data, ~1 PB of data-about-data) · ② Data COLLECTORS (collect from devices/users; e.g., cable TV tracking, RFID cart-path tracking) · ③ Data AGGREGATORS (compile + make sense of data from devices/websites/agencies; package for ad brokers) · ④ Data USERS & BUYERS (directly benefit; e.g., retail banks buying equity/credit data). [D] (EMC)
3. **THREE ROLE CATEGORIES (McKinsey May 2011)** — ① Deep Analytical Talent (technically savvy; raw unstructured data at scale; advanced quant training — math, stats, ML; needs robust analytic sandbox; e.g., statisticians, economists, mathematicians, Data Scientist; US talent gap forecast 140,000–190,000 by 2018) · ② Data Savvy Professionals (less technical depth; base knowledge of data/work with DS; business analysts, market research, life scientists, ops/financial managers; gap 1.5M by 2018 ≈ 10× the DS gap) · ③ Technology & Data Enablers (technical expertise; provision/administer sandboxes; manage large-scale data architectures; computer engineering, programming, DB administration). [D] (McKinsey-quoted)
4. **Data Scientist — 3 recurring activity sets** — (a) reframe business problems as analytics challenges + diagnose which methods apply; (b) design, implement, deploy complex statistical / data-mining models on Big Data; (c) develop & communicate insights + actionable recommendations; articulate business value. [D] (EMC)
5. **Data Scientist — 5 skill sets** — quantitative (math/stats) · technical aptitude (software engineering, ML, programming) · skeptical mindset & critical thinking · curious & creative · communicative & collaborative. [D] (EMC)
6. **Hadoop mention** — "Using technologies such as Hadoop to perform natural language processing on unstructured, textual data from social media" (sentiment gauging; disaster tracking via geotagged tweets). Hadoop appears as an ecosystem TOOL example only; architecture detail NOT in this lesson. [D]-as-taught
7. **Analytics applications cited** — NLP on social media for sentiment (campaigns); hurricane tracking via which areas tweet first. [D] (EMC)
