## 1 · MODULE 1 — FOUNDATIONS

### 1.1 Key Events in Community Architecture `[D]-as-taught`
- **Industrial Revolution** (Great Britain ~1760 → Europe/N. America by 1840s) triggered rapid mass **urbanization**, producing functional, mass-produced housing and public space for growing working-class populations.
- **Urban Renewal (1930s)** — response to the **Great Depression** (1929 Wall Street crash; longest/severe downturn of modern history). UK & US governments used public funds for **slum clearance**, demolishing unsafe housing/tenements to generate jobs and build early public housing.
- **Community Architecture (UK) ≡ Community Development (US)** — treated as equal movements with a shared goal: **public participation through planning**. **Rod Hackney** helped residents save and rebuild neighborhoods; **Prince Charles** became its high-profile royal champion, promoting human-scale ideas against "soulless modernist" planning.
- **Local context — Intramuros**: a surviving Spanish **Medieval Fortified Town (1571)**; **Daniel Burnham** pushed to preserve its moat in his 1905 Manila masterplan.
- *Source anchor:* Nick Wates & Charles Knevitt, *Community Architecture: How People Can Build Their Own Environment* (1987).

### 1.2 Introductory Lecture — Fundamentals of Urban Design & Community Architecture `[D]-as-taught`
- **The vulnerable community ("Life Underground"):** extreme poverty, insecure land tenure, high-risk zones (riverbanks, coastal fringes, floodplains); a **neglected sector** (budget gaps, slow housing production, remote relocation sites lacking jobs); the **income-affordability gap**.
- **The 5 Development Sectors** (basis of the **Comprehensive Development Plan / CDP**):
  1. **Institutional** — government bodies, laws, public administration, civic rules.
  2. **Environmental** — ecological health, climate resilience, sustainable resource management.
  3. **Social** — well-being, equity, quality of life (public services, housing, health, education, community spaces).
  4. **Economic** — commercial/industrial/agricultural activity → employment, growth, financial sustainability.
  5. **Infrastructure** — physical systems, public facilities, utilities.
- **Urban vs. Design vs. Urban Design:** *Urban* = region surrounding a city (dense, non-agricultural); *Design* (Int'l Council of Design) = study/practice of the interaction between a "user" and the man-made environment; *Urban Design* = **the art and practice of shaping the physical form, look, and function of towns and cities.**
- **Urban Planning vs. Urban Design:** Planning = **macro** (entire cities/regions; land use, zoning, housing, transport, environmental policy; tools = laws, data, policy). Design = **human scale** (streets, plazas, parks, building placement; aesthetics, how people feel/move; tools = layouts, landscaping, furniture, lighting, 3D form).
- **Community Architecture** = a practice that creates awareness in society, involves **users in the design process**, and helps them differentiate **needs vs. aspirations**.
- **Community Masterplan — Macro setting** (holistic composition): **Residential · Commercial · Institutional**.
- **Community Masterplan — Micro setting (key components):**
  - **Land Use Planning** (strategic land allocation for living/working/recreation/open space)
  - **Code Compliance** (local codes, zoning, environmental regs)
  - **Open Spaces** (essential from the onset; inclusivity + wellness)
  - **Circulation** (road networks, pedestrian walkways, public transit)
  - **Infrastructure & Utilities** (water, electricity, drainage, comms — resilient/scalable)
  - **Cultural Identity** (growth + modernity while preserving identity)
- **Key Philippine laws (Code Compliance & Planning):**
  - `RA 7279` — Urban Development & Housing Act (1992): socialized housing, land acquisition
  - `PD 1308` — Environmental Planning Act (licensed environmental planners)
  - `RA 11201` — created **DHSUD** (Dept. of Human Settlements & Urban Development)
  - `PD 1096` — National Building Code (density, setbacks, zoning limits)
  - `PD 957` — Subdivision & Condominium Buyers' Protective Decree (open space, road widths)
  - `BP 344` — Accessibility Law
  - `PD 1185 / RA 9514` — Fire Code (fire buffers, road sizing, emergency access)
  - `PD 705` — Revised Forestry Code · `RA 9275` — Clean Water Act · `RA 7586 / RA 11038` — NIPAS Act
- **Technical Due Diligence:** risk assessment (compliance with zoning, codes, permits, fire); site analysis (topography/slope, floodplains/drainage, soil/geology, climate/microclimate, vegetation/ecology, circulation/access, infrastructure, views, opportunities); **tree tagging plan**; **traffic study**; **hydrology study**; **bathymetric study** (marine/coastal); surveys (verification, relocation, topographic).

### 1.3 Sociocultural Basis of Community Design `[D]-as-taught`
- Mantra: **"Design WITH people, not FOR people."** — *"A space reflects who we are."*
- Community design = the **physical translation of a community's culture, daily habits, and social values** (not just roads, zoning, lot boundaries).
- **Community design as a sociocultural system** — views communities as living networks where behavior, values, structures, and spaces interact:
  - **Culture** — shared beliefs, values, customs, behaviors, objects, passed down generations.
  - **Society** (Cambridge) — a large group living together in an organized way, making decisions and sharing work.
  - **System** — working parts that connect and act together.
- **Sociocultural factors in design:** Demographics (age, population, education) · Social Organization (family/tribal/class → private vs. communal zoning) · History & Heritage (landmarks, land use, ancestral ties → identity) · Lifestyles & Customs (routines, food-sharing, markets → plazas, pathways, street life).
- **The sociocultural pillars:**
  1. **Social Capital & Spatial Interaction** — design can encourage/inhibit interaction; shared plazas, wide walkways, parks act as **"third places"** (outside home & work) where informal ties + mutual trust develop.
  2. **Cultural Identity** — architecture as physical archive of history, rituals, identity; vernacular forms + indigenous materials → authentic "spirit of place."
  3. **Expression of *Genius Loci*** — Latin, **"the spirit of a place"**: the unique atmosphere, character, inner meaning of a location.
  4. **Territoriality** — cultures hold distinct definitions of personal space, privacy, public life.
- **Philippine context:** **Fluid boundaries** (permeable public–private thresholds) · **Sari-sari store & *kanto* culture** (informal meet-up hub; exchange news, watch kids, build social trust — *kapwa*) · **street-level socialization** (*kanto* = corner, *beranda* = porch, *eskinita* = alley) — design must provide wide, shaded, walkable edges rather than high perimeter walls. Key values: **Bayanihan, Salo-salo, Kapwa**.

### 1.4 Orientation & Identity in Community Architecture `[D]-as-taught`
- **The architect's role = Collaborator AND Designer:** facilitate participation (workshops, surveys, town halls) · bridge gaps (translate technical/zoning language for everyday people) · promote inclusivity (accessible public space, shared facilities) · source locally (local materials/methods, lower cost, regional identity).
- **Participatory planning insights:** Resource Access (how people reach utilities/services) · Community Analysis (practices/traditions → spatial design) · Focus Group Discussions (bridging technical knowledge with community mission/vision) · Force Field Analysis (identifying factors that affect the project, aligning community interest with design).
- **Space and Place — Yi-Fu Tuan ("Father of Humanistic Geography"):** *"Space is freedom, place is security."* Space = coordinates/distance/empty dimension; Place = security, identity, belonging through lived experience.
- **Types of architectural forms / spatial organization:** linear (arranged about a straight line — directs movement, social hierarchy) · grouped/clustered (overlapped — shapes how people navigate, interact, perceive collective identity).
- **Wayfinding** ("Where am I?"): sightlines, visual axes, sensory wayfinding, clear thresholds, intuitive circulation. **Sensory cues:** Tactile (raised floor patterns, textured walls, Braille) · Auditory (water features, chimes, sound beacons) · Olfactory (scent zones) · Visual/Lighting (high-contrast paths, color coding). **Environmental Graphics Design (EGD)** = graphic design + architecture + lighting to connect people to places (malls, offices, residences, schools).
- **Laws of the Indies (*Leyes de Indias*, 1573)** — royal ordinances of **King Philip II of Spain**: the **first standardized global planning code**, dictating site selection, grid layouts (*cuadrícula*), building dimensions, street orientation. Comprises: **Plaza (Plaza Mayor) · Government palace (Cabildo) · Church · Residential areas** — the Spanish colonial town template (context for Intramuros).

### 1.5 Elements of Urban Design — *Image of the City* (Kevin Lynch) `[D]-as-taught`
How people mentally perceive, navigate, and remember cities. **The five elements:**

| Element | Role | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Paths** | channels of movement | Champs-Élysées; "Great Streets" (people-first right-of-way); Las Ramblas, Barcelona |
| **Edges** | linear boundaries separating zones | natural (rivers/forests/coastlines — Colorado River), artificial (highways/rails/walls — Great Wall), perceptual (shifts in style/land use — BGC–Cembo) |
| **Districts** | medium-large areas of distinct character | Akihabara; Chinatown, Singapore |
| **Nodes** | strategic junctions / focal points | Shibuya; The Bund–Nanjing Road; intersections, transit hubs, public spaces |
| **Landmarks** | point-reference objects (collective memory & identity) | Sagrada Familia; Eiffel Tower — via visibility, uniqueness, significance |

- **Responsive Environment** — *Responsive Environments: A Manual for Designers* (Ian Bentley): a space maximizing **user choice, adaptability, fluid experience**. **Seven qualities:** Connectivity · Variety · Legibility · Robustness · Visual Appropriateness · Richness · Personalization.

### 1.6 Urban Design Theories `[D]-as-taught`
- **Garden City Movement — Ebenezer Howard (1898/1902):** balance city + nature. **Three Magnets** — *Town* (jobs, society, high wages but slums/foul air/high rents) · *Country* (beauty, fresh air, low rents but no jobs/infrastructure) · ***Town-Country* (the Garden City)** — combines both. Principles: limited size/population density · encircling **greenbelt** · radial (wheel-and-spoke) design + fair land distribution. **Letchworth Garden City** (Hertfordshire, England; Barry Parker & Raymond Unwin): radial civic core, >⅔ land as permanent agricultural belt, low-density Arts-&-Crafts cottages, self-contained economy.
- **Concentric Zone Model — Ernest Burgess (1925, Chicago School):** five rings radiating from the CBD — (1) CBD, (2) zone of transition, (3) independent workers' homes, (4) better residences, (5) commuter zone. Growth via **invasion & succession** (zones transition land-use/social groups). *"The Target Board."*
- **Sector Theory — Homer Hoyt (1939):** city grows along **transportation corridors** in **pie-shaped wedges/sectors** (high/low-status by land value & amenities). *"The Pizza Slices."*
- **Multi-Nuclei Theory — Chauncy Harris & Edward Ullman (1945), *The Nature of Cities*:** cities develop **multiple centers/nuclei**, each specialized, interdependent, linked by transport. *"The Multi-Hub City."*
- **Neighborhood Unit Concept — Clarence Perry (1929):** ~**160-acre** self-sufficient neighborhood centered on a school; pedestrian-friendly, **hierarchical roads** (arterials on the perimeter, quiet local streets inside), integrated parks/green space. *"The Walkable Village."*
- **City Beautiful Movement — Daniel Burnham (1893 World's Columbian Exposition, the "White City"):** neoclassical aesthetic order, civic/cultural centers, grand boulevards/parks, balancing function with beauty.

## 2 · MODULE 2 — PROCESS & HOUSING TYPOLOGIES

### 2.1 Urban Design Process (Critical Evaluation of Urban Environments) `[D]-as-taught`
- **Definition:** urban design is a **negotiation among parties through political and economic means** — a collaborative process linking ideas.
- **Planning scales:** **Barangay** (immediate human needs, daily comfort) · **City/Municipality** (zoning, transport lines, commercial zones) · **Regional** (shared resources — water, electricity across towns/provinces) · **National** (broad sectoral vision, resource protection/allocation).
- **The 3 phases:**
  - **Phase 1 — Preliminary Studies:** data collection, on-site surveys, review of existing plans · review of current policy environment (laws, policies, frameworks, projects) · identify development **constraints & opportunities** · identify development **issues & concerns**. *(Transition 1→2: a **Community Visioning Workshop** / participatory planning defines shared goals.)*
  - **Phase 2 — Concept Formulation:** identify key **spatial strategies** → formulate **alternative plans** → **select plans** via the **Goals Achievement Matrix (GAM)** — a decision tool ranking/prioritizing **programs, projects & activities (PPAs)** for the CDP (national → local scale).
  - **Phase 3 — Detailed Planning:** technical drawings (conceptual → schematic → design development → bid → for-construction) & estimates → **construction & bidding** → **implementation** → **post-implementation review**.
- **Participatory planning tools** (methods to engage community/stakeholders; ensure projects match real local needs, improve data accuracy, build trust via transparency):

| Tool | Description | Key identifier | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual Survey** | evaluation of form & composition of an area | direct observation | physical character/aesthetics pre-planning |
| **Community Mapping** | participants share insights & locate resources on maps | spatial knowledge | hazards, informal boundaries |
| **Design Charrettes** | collaborative workshop | brainstorming | immediate design solutions |
| **Transect Walks** | participants walk together to observe site conditions | ocular visits | spatial transitions (ridge-to-reef) |
| **Participatory Appraisal** | community analyzes its own priorities/decisions | assessment | sectoral diagnosis, baseline assessments |

### 2.2 Cluster Housing & Planned Unit Developments (PUD) `[D]-as-taught`
- **Cluster housing** — a single-family **attached** dwelling of **3+ separate living units** grouped compactly. Origin: medieval; formal recognition 20th century as an alternative to suburban sprawl; first applied at **Radburn, New Jersey (1928)**, influenced by the English **Garden City** movement.
- **PUD** — a site where residential/commercial/industrial (or combinations) may be authorized **flexibly** to achieve the goals of a municipal comprehensive plan. (In the Philippines, PUD evolved through urbanization, land-use planning, housing needs — no single dedicated historical timeline.)
- **Types of cluster housing:** Traditional · **Pocket Neighborhood** (homes around shared courtyard/pathway) · **Eco-villages** (intentional, minimal environmental impact, shared green/renewable resources) · **Co-housing** (private dwellings + shared kitchens/dining, participatory decision-making) · **Transit-Oriented Development / TOD** (mixed-use near transit hubs, reduced car reliance — source: DHSUD) · **Agrihoods** (homes around working farms/gardens) · **Senior co-housing** (aging-in-place, shared amenities) · **Mixed-income cluster** (diverse housing types/price levels → socioeconomic diversity).
- **PUD forms:** **Mixed-use development** (residential+commercial+recreational, walkable) · **Master-planned communities** (multiple neighborhoods on large properties) · **Urban infill development** (revitalizing underutilized urban spaces) · **Industrial park** (designated zone for factories/warehouses/shipping — separates industry from neighborhoods, near highways/rail/ports).
- **Areas for Priority Development** (per the Supreme Court of the Philippines): specific **blighted, underutilized, or informal-settlement zones** targeted for land reform, infrastructure upgrading, and socialized housing. **Criteria:** *Blighted conditions* (dilapidated/obsolete/unsanitary structures) · *Depressed status* (slum community needing land reform/upgrading/socialized housing) · *Special economic zones* (separate customs territories, tax incentives, duty-free imports).

1. **Participation is the through-line** — from Rod Hackney to design charrettes to FGDs: community architecture is defined by *involving users*.
2. **Scale ladder** — barangay → city → regional → national; and macro (masterplan) → micro (site components).
3. **The Filipino spatial DNA** — fluid boundaries, *kapwa*, sari-sari/kanto, Intramuros/Laws of the Indies lineage.
4. **Theory timeline** — Howard (1898) → Burgess (1925) → Perry (1929) → Hoyt (1939) → Harris & Ullman (1945), plus the City Beautiful movement (1893) — know each model's shape ("target board," "pizza slices," "multi-hub").
5. **The two design lenses** — Lynch's *Image of the City* (5 elements of perception) vs. Bentley's *Responsive Environments* (7 qualities of good space).
6. **Legal spine** — RA 7279 / PD 1096 / PD 957 / BP 344 / PD 1185 / RA 11201 (DHSUD) / PD 1308 / NIPAS / Clean Water / Forestry codes.
