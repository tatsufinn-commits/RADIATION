# GED103 Syllabus Q1 2026–2027




COURSE SYLLABUS

Course Code: GED103

Course Title: Readings in Philippine History

Prerequisite: None

Co-Requisite: None

Credit: 3 units

Course Description: The course analyzes Philippine history from multiple perspectives through the lens of selected primary sources coming from various disciplines and different genres. Students are given opportunities to analyze the author’s background and main arguments, compare different points of view, identify biases and examine the evidence presented in the document. The discussions will tackle traditional topics in history and other interdisciplinary themes that will deepen and broaden their understanding of Philippine politics, economics, cultural, social, scientific and religious history. Priority is given to primary materials that could help students develop their analytical and communications skills. The end goal is to develop the historical and critical consciousness of the students so that they will become versatile, articulate, broad-minded, morally upright, and responsible citizens. This course includes mandatory topics on the Philippine Constitution, agrarian reform, and taxation

## Policy on the Use of AI Tools and Technologies

Mapúa University recognizes artificial intelligence (AI) as an increasingly important tool in learning, professional practice, research, and innovation. Consistent with the University's AI Fluency Strategy, students are expected to develop the ability to use AI responsibly within their discipline, evaluate AI-generated outputs critically, and exercise human judgment in all academic work.

The AI Use Classification assigned to each assessment task shall govern the extent to which AI tools may be used in the course:

AI-Prohibited assessments require students to demonstrate foundational knowledge, skills, and competencies without AI assistance.
AI-Assisted assessments allow the use of AI tools, provided that such use is properly disclosed and documented by the student.
AI-Integrated assessments expect the use of AI tools as part of authentic professional or disciplinary practice.

Regardless of the classification, students remain fully accountable for the accuracy, quality, originality, ethical use, and academic integrity of all submitted work. AI-generated content shall not be represented as the student's own intellectual effort.

When required by the instructor, students may be asked to provide evidence of their learning process, including prompts, drafts, revision histories, reflection notes, design rationale, annotated outputs, or other documentation demonstrating how AI tools were used and how human judgment was applied.

The use of AI tools for plagiarism, fabrication of information, falsification of data, unauthorized completion of assessments, or any other form of academic dishonesty is strictly prohibited and shall be subject to the University's Academic Integrity Policy.

DLA Statement on the Use of Generative AI Tools and Technologies

The Department of Liberal Arts is among the pillars of world-class Mapúa University education. It advocates the responsible, reasonable, lawful, and ethical use of tools, such as Generative Artificial Intelligence (Generative AI), made available by technology to promote a rich and meaningful teaching and learning experience. Such use is regulated by relevant policies set by the Department, the School, the University, and other entities – internal or external to the University – tasked to oversee the use of these tools.

Responsible use entails, among others:

fact-checking;
correct citations;
full disclosure of AI-generated material used, properly cited.  Your teacher shall provide you with a copy of the disclosure form; and,
the use of AI-generated material in any and all tasks is limited to 10% for undergraduate work.

Reasonable use includes but is not limited to:

the use of AI on tasks where the teacher explicitly allows it;
refraining from using AI for personal essays such as insight papers, reflection papers, and the like.

Lawful and ethical use refers to abiding by established legal and ethical boundaries of AI use.

It shall be the teacher’s prerogative to subject student submissions to AI detector and similar tools.

Professional courses under the BS Technical Communication Program, PCC150, and specific English aand Communication courses required by CINTANA follow the blended modality: face-to-face and asynchronous/synchronous.

Attendance.

Based on the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) policy, a student who accumulates absences equivalent to 20% of the prescribed number of school days in one trimester (or a maximum of 7 absences for 3-unit courses), shall automatically be given a final grade of “5” (FAILED).

For fully online courses held asynchronously thrice during odd weeks, and synchronously twice every even weeks, the maximum number of absences is set as follows:


For courses run on blended modality, the maximum number of absences is 7.

For UOx courses held fully asynchronously, the maximum number of absences is 7.

When attending synchronous classes, a student should be in a conducive study environment such as a work or study area, a classroom, or a suitable area at home.

A student is considered absent from asynchronous classes if no output was submitted for asynchronous tasks.  Instructors shall open the asynchronous tasks on a Monday for MWF classes, and on a Tuesday of the TTHS classes.  Students need to accomplish the asynchronous tasks by Thursday of the class week for MWF classes, and by Friday of the class week for TTHS classes.


Instructors have the option to open and close tasks earlier than schedule, as discussed with students.

A student is considered absent from synchronous classes if he/she fails to:

attend the class from a safe and conducive place; the student should not be in transit (e.g. walking, driving, commuting);
secure a stable internet connection unless due to a validated issue with the internet provider; and,
keep his camera on for the whole duration of the class.

A student’s absence is considered excused when it meets the following conditions and requirements:

If the absence is due to medical reasons, the student is required to submit to the instructor before attending the next scheduled class, a medical certificate duly validated by the University Physician and clearly indicating the period of illness.
The medical certificate should indicate that the student’s condition prevents him/her from attending online classes.
If the absence is due to having to attend an institutional, school, or department activity, an excuse letter signed by OSAAR (for institutional activities), or signed by the DLA Department Chair (for school and department activities) must be submitted by the following meeting.
If the absence is due to a medical emergency such as confinement or major injuries, the same document is required as stipulated in Parts 3 a and b of this section.
If the absence is due to internet issues, power outage, or similar circumstances, an official statement from the service provider stating the scope and date of such issues or outage must be submitted to the instructor by the following meeting.
In the case of death of an immediate family (up to second degree consanguinity)
If the absence is due to mental health reasons, a written certificate signed by the University Guidance Counselor must be submitted to the instructor by the next meeting the student will attend.

All other cases not specified in this section are subject to the instructor’s discretion and the applicable University policy.

Academic Integrity Policy.

It is the student’s responsibility to refrain from infractions of academic integrity, from conduct that may lead to suspicion of such infractions, and from conduct which aids others to commit such infractions. Any of the following sanctions may be imposed on any student who is found guilty of committing any form of academic dishonesty – online or otherwise.

failed mark in the course
suspension for a period of less than one term, with or without community service
suspension for a period of one term or more, with or without community service
non-readmission to the University
dismissal from the University
expulsion

The following are considered violations of academic dishonesty:

using another myMapua email address to login to any platform (such as BlackBoard and Coursera) with or without permission;
asking or hiring someone else to do their exams, homework, Coursera course, papers, projects or other academic requirements;
recording and saving copies of exam questions or answers, or answer keys for distribution;
receiving copies of exam questions or answers, or answer keys to an exam;
plagiarizing or the unethical act of stealing the ideas of another without proper citation or reference, acquiring information from the Internet without acknowledging the author, copying from another student’s work without permission and submitting it as one’s own work;
generating AI material without proper disclosure or accurate citation;
massive, pre-meditated, organized cheating (online or otherwise) using instant messaging/email during a quiz or exam;
any form of dishonesty in peer-reviewed assignments/submissions (e.g. Coursera peer-graded submissions);
engaging in any activities that will dishonestly improve results, or dishonestly improve or damage the outputs of others; and,
any other form of dishonesty or cheating in any assessment or course requirement.

A student who violates the Academic Integrity Policy of the University will be given a zero mark for the exam or the activity and will be given a failing grade for the course. He or she will also be referred to the Prefect of Discipline for appropriate sanction.

Appeal system.

All appeals regarding student assessment and grades must be submitted directly to the instructor by the student concerned at the end of each module.  Appeals must be made at the end of each module, as follows: Week 4 (for Module 1), Week 8 (for Module 2), and until Thursday of Week 11 (for Module 3). Specific schedules shall be announced by the instructor.

In case the student is not satisfied with the results of the appeal, he or she can elevate the appeal to Department Chair, no later than one week after the decision of the instructor has been made. The decision of the Department Chair is final. Instructors and students must abide by the moderated decision of the Department Chair.

Grade of 5.00

A grade of “5.00” is given to a student whose performance is poor, according to the following guidelines:

a.  has a weighted average below 70%;
b.  stopped attending classes at any time without officially withdrawing through the procedure set by the Office of the Registrar;
has accumulated absences equivalent to 20% or more of the prescribed number of school days in one (1) term, according to Item 16 B of this syllabus.  After this number of absences has been recorded, a final grade of 5.00 is given regardless of class standing; and,
d.  violated the Academic Integrity policies of the University.

A grade of “5.00” for a course within the program of study shall disqualify a student from academic scholarship and academic honors.

Grade of “I” (INCOMPLETE)

The grade of “I” is equivalent to an incomplete grade and is not a mark of conditional failure.

A grade of “I” is given under the following conditions:

a.  The student was not able to take the final examination for a justifiable reason, provided that his/her class standing is passing before the final examination.
b.  In case of extraordinary or special circumstance, the Dean or Department Chair can approve the request, on a case-to-case basis, after weighing all factors and considerations which would properly justify the giving of a grade of “I”.

A grade of “I” for a course outside the program of study of a student shall not affect his/her scholarship privileges (except for eligibility in the academic scholarship).

A grade of “I” for a course within the program of study shall disqualify a student from his/her academic scholarship.

A grade of “I” shall not be included in the computation of the general weighted average for a particular quarter.

No general weighted average shall be reflected in the grade report if all courses for a particular quarter are given a grade of “I”.

A grade of “I” must be completed within the next quarter for undergraduate programs.

If a student fails to complete the requirements within the prescribed period, the grade of “I” lapses and the student shall be required to repeat the course. During the prescribed period of completion, the student may opt to re-enroll the course in lieu of completing the requirements; in such case, he/she will have to repeat the same course and satisfy all its requirements. The student shall inform his/her Section Chief at the Registrar’s Office of the intent to re-enroll the course and accomplish the necessary documentation.

Requirements for the completion of a grade of “I”:

a.  For regular fully online General Education courses and blended BSTC professional courses and other identified courses, the student shall submit a duly accomplished Request to Complete Course Form (FM-RO-19) to the Customer Service for billing and processing of Completion Report Form (FM-RO-20). The student shall submit the CRF to the Instructor who will fill out the form, and submit it to the Department Chair for signing. The completely signed CRF shall be forwarded to the Registrar’s Office for encoding.
b.  For UOx General Education courses, the CRF signed by the instructor and Dean/Department Chair concerned should be submitted to the Office of the Registrar for encoding.
