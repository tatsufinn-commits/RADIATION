## WK 1 · KEY EVENTS + FUNDAMENTALS
**SUMMARY:** Mass urbanization began with the **Industrial Revolution** (GB ~1760), which produced functional mass housing for a new working class. The **Great Depression** (1929 crash) led to **Urban Renewal** in the 1930s — UK/US used public funds for **slum clearance**. **Community Architecture (UK)** and **Community Development (US)** are treated as equal movements sharing one goal: **public participation through planning**, championed by **Rod Hackney** and **Prince Charles**. Locally, **Intramuros (1571)** is the surviving Spanish fortified town whose moat **Daniel Burnham** fought to keep in his 1905 Manila plan. The fundamentals sheet then defines urban design, the vulnerable community, the 5 development sectors, the macro/micro masterplan, the legal spine, and technical due-diligence studies.

**ID-HOTSPOTS (term → context):**
- **Industrial Revolution** — GB ~1760; trigger of mass urbanization.
- **Urban Renewal** — 1930s slum-clearance response to the Depression.
- **Rod Hackney** — British architect; residents save/rebuild own neighborhoods.
- **Prince Charles** — royal champion vs "soulless modernist" planning.
- **Wates & Knevitt** — 1987 anchor book authors.
- **Intramuros** — 1571 Spanish medieval fortified town, Manila.
- **Daniel Burnham** — 1905 Manila masterplan; preserve Intramuros moat.
- **Income-affordability gap** — earnings vs essential living costs.

| Must-recall | Content |
|---|---|
| **5 Development Sectors (CDP basis)** | Institutional · Environmental · Social · Economic · Infrastructure |
| **Urban design (def.)** | art & practice of shaping physical form, look, function of towns/cities |
| **Planning vs design scale** | planning = **macro**; design = **human** scale |
| **Masterplan MACRO** | Residential · Commercial · Institutional |
| **Masterplan MICRO (6)** | Land Use · Code Compliance · Open Spaces · Circulation · Infrastructure & Utilities · Cultural Identity |
| **Due diligence** | **tree tagging** (trees affected) · **traffic** study · **hydrology** · **bathymetric** (marine/coastal) · surveys: verification / **relocation** (lost markers) / topographic |
| **Vulnerable community** | extreme poverty · insecure tenure · high-risk zones (riverbanks/coastal/floodplains) |

## WK 2 · SOCIOCULTURAL BASIS
**SUMMARY:** Community design is the **physical translation of a community's culture, habits, and values** — captured by the mantra "Design **WITH** people, not **FOR** people." It rests on sociocultural pillars: **Social Capital & Spatial Interaction** (plazas/wide walkways as **"third places"**), **Cultural Identity** (architecture as physical archive), and **Territoriality**, all expressing the **Genius Loci** ("spirit of a place"). In the Philippine setting this means **fluid boundaries**, the **sari-sari store / kanto** as informal hub, and values of **Bayanihan, Salo-salo, Kapwa** — so design provides **wide, shaded, walkable edges**, NOT high perimeter walls.

**ID-HOTSPOTS:**
- **Genius loci** — Latin "spirit of a place."
- **Third places** — informal tie-builders outside home & work.
- **Kapwa** — Filipino shared humanity / social trust.
- **Sari-sari store / kanto** — informal meet-up hub (*kanto*=corner, *beranda*=porch, *eskinita*=alley).
- **Mantra** — "Design WITH people, not FOR people."

| Must-recall | Content |
|---|---|
| **3 sociocultural pillars** | Social Capital & Spatial Interaction · Cultural Identity · Territoriality |
| **Sociocultural factors** | Demographics · Social Organization · History & Heritage · Lifestyles & Customs |
| **PH design directive** | wide/shaded/walkable edges, NOT high perimeter walls |

## WK 3 · ORIENTATION & IDENTITY
**SUMMARY:** The architect acts as **Collaborator AND Designer** — facilitating participation, translating technical language, and sourcing locally. Orientation/identity draws on **Yi-Fu Tuan** ("Space is freedom, place is security"; Father of Humanistic Geography), **wayfinding** via four sensory cues, and **EGD**. The historical template is the **Laws of the Indies (1573)** of **King Philip II** — the first standardized global planning code (grid *cuadrícula*), composed of Plaza, Government palace, Church, and Residential areas.

**ID-HOTSPOTS:**
- **Yi-Fu Tuan** — "Space is freedom, place is security"; Father of Humanistic Geography.
- **EGD** — Environmental Graphics Design.
- **Laws of the Indies** — 1573; King Philip II; first standardized global planning code.
- **Cabildo** — government palace in the Indies town.

| Must-recall | Content |

## WK 4 · ELEMENTS + THEORIES
**SUMMARY:** Two lenses dominate. **Kevin Lynch** (*Image of the City*, 1960) describes how people **perceive** the city through **five elements** (Paths, Edges, Districts, Nodes, Landmarks). **Ian Bentley** (*Responsive Environments*, 1985) prescribes **seven qualities** of good space. Six classic **urban-design theories** give each model a memorable shape and date — know author + year + shape.

**ID-HOTSPOTS (shape → theory):** "three magnets"=Garden City · "target board"=Concentric Zone · "walkable village"=Neighborhood Unit · "pizza slices"=Sector · "multi-hub"=Multi-Nuclei · "White City"=City Beautiful.

| Element/Quality/Theory | Recall |
|---|---|
| **Lynch 5** | Paths · Edges · Districts · Nodes · Landmarks |
| **Edge types** | natural (Colorado R.) · artificial (Great Wall) · perceptual (BGC–Cembo) |
| **Bentley 7** | Connectivity · Variety · Legibility · Robustness · Visual Appropriateness · Richness · Personalization |
| **Garden City** | Howard 1898/1902 · greenbelt · radial · Letchworth (Parker & Unwin) |
| **City Beautiful** | Burnham 1893 · neoclassical White City |
| **Concentric Zone** | Burgess 1925 · 5 rings · invasion & succession |
| **Neighborhood Unit** | Perry 1929 · ~160 ac · school-centered |
| **Sector** | Hoyt 1939 · transport-corridor wedges |
| **Multi-Nuclei** | Harris & Ullman 1945 · *The Nature of Cities* |

⚑ Bentley's original first term = **Permeability**; lecture uses **Connectivity** (curriculum term wins on MC).

## WK 5 · URBAN DESIGN PROCESS
**SUMMARY:** Urban design is a **negotiation among parties through political and economic means**, read across four **planning scales** (Barangay → City/Municipality → Regional → National). The **3 phases** run Preliminary Studies → (Community Visioning Workshop) → Concept Formulation (select via **GAM**) → Detailed Planning (drawings → bidding → implementation → post-review). Five **participatory tools** engage the community.

**ID-HOTSPOTS:**
- **GAM** — Goals Achievement Matrix; ranks PPAs for the CDP.
- **Community Visioning Workshop** — the Phase 1→2 transition.
- **Transect Walks** — ocular; ridge-to-reef transitions.
- **Design Charrettes** — collaborative brainstorm workshop.

| Must-recall | Content |
|---|---|
| **4 scales** | Barangay · City/Municipality · Regional · National |
| **3 phases** | Preliminary Studies · Concept Formulation · Detailed Planning |
| **Drawing stages (5)** | conceptual → schematic → design development → bid → for-construction |
| **5 tools** | Visual Survey · Community Mapping · Design Charrettes · Transect Walks · Participatory Appraisal |

## WK 6 · CLUSTER HOUSING & PUD
**SUMMARY:** **Cluster housing** = a single-family **attached** dwelling of **3+ units** grouped compactly; first applied at **Radburn, NJ (1928)** under **Garden City** influence. A **PUD** flexibly combines residential/commercial/industrial to meet the comprehensive plan. Know the **8 cluster types**, **4 PUD forms**, and the Supreme Court's **3 criteria** for Areas for Priority Development.

| Must-recall | Content |
|---|---|
| **8 cluster types** | Traditional · Pocket Neighborhood · Eco-villages · Co-housing · TOD · Agrihoods · Senior co-housing · Mixed-income |
| **4 PUD forms** | Mixed-use · Master-planned communities · Urban infill · Industrial park |
| **Priority Dev. (3 criteria)** | Blighted conditions · Depressed status · Special economic zones |
| **Agrihood / TOD** | around working farms / near transit hubs |
